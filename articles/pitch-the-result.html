Meaning as code · what it's for

Answers you can defend — from your own data.

An AI that answers from what your data means, not just what it stores: it shows its reasoning, refuses what it can't ground, and gets sharper each time you teach it — in plain language, by the people who know the business.

Early-stage concept · working proof-of-concept on public data · seeking seed funding

What it gives you

the result, not the plumbing

  • One number everyone agrees on
    "Revenue", "margin", "active customer" mean one agreed thing — so the standing argument over whose figure is right can finally end.
  • Answers that show their work
    Every number comes with the rule it followed and what it assumed. You can take it into the room and defend it — or challenge it.
  • It won't make things up
    When a question is ambiguous it asks; when the data can't support an answer it says so. No confident wrong number that costs you three weeks.
  • Knowledge that doesn't leave
    What lives in your best analyst's head gets written down once, in plain words — and stays when they move on.
  • It compounds
    Teach it a new definition once and it holds — for everyone, from then on. It grows with the business instead of ageing against it.

What already works

on a public demo — a real model, every number executed

  • ✓
    Gets the contested number right. Asked for "total sales", it converts five currencies and nets discount — and discloses both. The exact call a naive text-to-SQL bot gets silently wrong.
  • ✓
    Refuses instead of inventing. Asked for a profit it can't ground, it declines and says "profit isn't defined — define it first", rather than returning a plausible wrong figure.
  • ✓
    Grows from one plain-language edit. Author that definition in a small file, and the same question now answers — reasoning disclosed — with automatic checks confirming it's sound.
  • ✓
    Portable by design. The meaning is captured once, independent of any one database, tool or vendor — free to run on what you already own.
Where it honestly is. A proof of concept, not a product. The tooling is young and the tested set of questions is small — but the core loop works end-to-end on real data, and the approach rests on the ordinary disciplines of software: version it, review it, test it, check it.
